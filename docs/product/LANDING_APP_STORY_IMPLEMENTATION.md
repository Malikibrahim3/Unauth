## 2. Design for the merchant who knows nothing about Unauth

Primary visitor: an ecommerce founder or support/operations lead who already has a store platform, helpdesk and fulfilment process. They have encountered refund disputes, missing evidence, duplicate requests, uncertain responsibility or reimbursements nobody followed up. They are evaluating whether another tool will remove work or add it. They have not read PRODUCT and do not know what “the gate” means.

Secondary visitors: a recovery operator who needs the next concrete action, and a finance lead who needs to distinguish money expected from money received. Mobile visitors can evaluate the product; the functional workspace and interactive case remain desktop-only.

| Moment | Merchant question | Required answer on the page |
|---|---|---|
| First glance | What is this, and is it for a business like mine? | Ecommerce claims and eligible recovery, in a shared operations workspace. |
| First short scroll | Why would I add it to the tools I already use? | The order, conversation, evidence, decisions, tasks and financial result stay connected. Existing systems remain part of the workflow. |
| Product overview | What does the app actually let us do? | Recognisable Cases, Recovery, Work, Reconciliation, Reports and Rules jobs, each shown through a specific action and output. |
| Detailed reading | How does a case move between those jobs? | The same evidence supports a decision and separate recovery work; ownership follows the work; receipts connect to the financial record. |
| Trust check | What is automatic, what is manual, and what is available? | An explicit current offer and local distinctions for manual external actions, pre-launch AI and planned additions. |
| Decision to continue | What happens if I click? | A clearly named existing destination, accurate desktop expectation, and no surprise sales form or account commitment. |

Use these as design questions, not public page headings verbatim. Never require a visitor to understand liability modelling, fixture versions, certification profiles or reconciliation internals before grasping the value.

## 12. Validate with actual first-time merchants

This is a prospective acceptance protocol, not a claim of completed research or a statistically representative conversion study. Use five people who make or materially influence ecommerce support, operations, recovery or finance decisions, who have not previously used Unauth. Include at least one hands-on support/operations person and one finance/recovery person. Record relevant experience, device and prior exposure; do not require sensitive business records.

The owner supplies participants or explicitly authorises recruitment. This document does not authorise messages to people, incentives or publication of participant data. If participants are unavailable, finish the independently reviewable local work and record this gate PENDING.

### Session script

1. **Five-second impression:** show the first viewport, then hide it. Ask “What do you think this product does, and who is it for?” Do not mention claims, recovery or connected tools in the prompt.
2. **Thirty-second scan:** allow an unassisted short scroll. Ask “Why might your business add this to the tools it already uses?” and “What would you expect your team to do in it?”
3. **Two-minute exploration:** let the person read freely. Ask them to explain how one customer problem connects to recovery work and money. Ask which parts support, operations and finance would use.
4. **Task finding:** ask them to find where they would learn about missing evidence/next work, checking whether money came back, and reviewing recurring issues. Record first destination, hesitation and misconceptions; do not coach them towards a label.
5. **Trust and action:** ask “What can you use today?”, “What happens automatically?”, “What does your team still do elsewhere?”, and “What will happen if you click the main action?” Ask them to distinguish the illustrated recovery ceiling from cash received.
6. **Open response:** ask what is unclear, what feels repetitive and what information they still need to evaluate fit. Record their own words before explaining the intended design.

Score unaided answers against a prewritten answer key derived from verified scope. Do not reward parroting a heading or retroactively redefine success after hearing responses.

### Prospective pass criteria

- At least **4 of 5** identify both the claims/decision and eligible-recovery purpose and recognise the ecommerce audience.
- At least **4 of 5** explain the value of shared context across existing tools, rather than describing Unauth only as another helpdesk or a refund checker.
- At least **4 of 5** name three concrete product jobs, including a job beyond the immediate customer decision.
- At least **4 of 5** can connect evidence, decision, separate recovery work and a verified financial outcome in their own words.
- At least **4 of 5** find the relevant explanation for each task without coaching and correctly predict the main CTA destination and desktop requirement.
- **All 5** avoid material false conclusions about guaranteed collection, automatic refunds/claims/messages, available public AI enforcement, or a ceiling/approval being money received. Any such inference requires a targeted correction and a new unprimed check.
- The current offer is correctly understood by **all 5**. If the offer itself remains unverified, this criterion cannot pass.

These thresholds are chosen quality gates for this project, not published research findings. Save anonymised prompts, answers, task observations, scoring and failures. Distinguish editorial comprehension improvement from conversion, time savings or production business impact.
