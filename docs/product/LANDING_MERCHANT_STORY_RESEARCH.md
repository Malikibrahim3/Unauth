## Recommendation

**Sell the connected job: resolve the customer's problem, control the action and pursue the merchant's money back without rebuilding the case between teams and tools.**

The last revisions confused concise presentation with a smaller product. We removed repetition, but also removed the connective explanation that makes the collection of capabilities worth buying. The current page explains individual refund decisions and a prepared recovery task better than it explains the operating system around them. A merchant can understand the pictured case and still not understand why to adopt Unauth.

The answer is a richer sequence of short, distinct chapters. Each needs a recognisable merchant problem, a visible change in how the work happens, and a specific reason to care. More narrative coverage does not require more paragraphs per section.

The organising promise is:

> Less work between the tools. More control over decisions. A clear path to eligible money back.

This is proposed positioning for the **finished, supported product** described in [PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md). It is not a claim that every proposed operation is available today. The existing supervised product and the intended connected product need different availability statements, not different underlying reasons to exist.

**My inference:** the strongest buyer is an established goods merchant whose support process can resolve an issue but whose team still reconstructs context, coordinates exceptions, pursues reimbursements or checks settlement elsewhere. They buy relief from that recurring work and a more controlled result. Merchants whose existing setup already completes this job may not need Unauth. Research does not establish the segment's prevalence or willingness to pay.

The useful commercial mechanisms are specific:

- **Less handling:** reuse evidence and decisions instead of copying, rechecking and rewriting them. Measure touches and total operator minutes per supported case.
- **Better-controlled concessions:** check prior remedies, concurrent permissions and policy before acting. Measure substantiated conflicts and actual outcomes; a blocked amount is not automatically savings.
- **More complete eligible pursuit:** collect evidence early and keep valid claims moving. Measure incremental reconciled cash/applied credit against the merchant's existing recovery process, after costs.
- **Better customer handling:** compare feasible remedies and keep customer resolution independent of reimbursement. Measure resolution time, repeat contacts and complaints; retention is a separate hypothesis.
- **Reliable financial understanding:** connect action, claim and actual settlement. Measure unresolved allocations, collection time and remaining recorded balances.

Do not combine these into an invented ROI headline. Labour capacity is not necessarily payroll saved; recovered cash is not necessarily incremental; a one-off backlog is not recurring monthly value. The fixed subscription/no-recovery-percentage model can support the pricing argument, but price terms stay in the catalogue and the previous removal of repeated pricing copy remains respected.

## 5. Proposed page story and draft copy

This is one recommendation, not a list of alternative directions. The copy below describes the finished supported product. Before implementation, reconcile availability against released coverage as specified in section 8. It is not ready to paste into a current public launch page without that treatment.

### 0 — Open with the whole promise

**Keep the current headline:**

> Your rules before money moves. Recover what you're owed.

**Proposed supporting copy:**

> Bring the evidence, your team and connected AI into one workflow—from the customer request to a controlled resolution and verified money back.

Use the genuine full-frame Asterlane hero to establish that this is substantial working software. The subhead adds breadth that the headline alone cannot carry. Retain the walkthrough's actual destination and expectations. One concise availability statement identifies the current offer; it does not become the main benefit paragraph.

### 1 — Make the fragmented work recognisable, then connect it

**Before heading:**

> One customer issue. Work scattered everywhere.

**Before copy:**

> The conversation is in support. The refund is in your store. Warehouse records, delivery evidence and the money back follow their own paths.

**After heading:**

> One case. The whole team can move it forward.

**After copy:**

> Unauth connects the facts, the decision and the follow-through—so each team starts where the last one left off.

**Artefact:** a paired composition around the same order. Before: support/store already connected, separate warehouse record, carrier timeline, claim correspondence and payment evidence; visibly repeated gathering or handoff work. After: the same source objects join one Unauth case, which branches into customer resolution and eligible recovery, then links actual outcomes to finance. Do not create a decorative logo constellation. Show what travels across the connections: photo, prior refund, policy decision, claim reference and receipt.

**Takeaway:** “It joins up work my tools leave between them.” This is the opening platform explanation; it replaces the thin three-job strip as the main orientation.

### 2 — Give people and AI the same decision boundary

**Heading:**

> Your rules. Whoever handles the claim.

**Supporting copy:**

> People get a recommendation with reasons. Connected AI gets permission for a specific action—or a handoff to a person. Your rules govern both.

**Artefact:** human and connected-AI lanes enter the same gate alongside evidence and merchant policy. Show the outputs: permitted action, review required or evidence needed. In the human lane, an authorised exception has a named person and reason. In the AI lane, an exception returns to human review. One compact second detail shows a concession already recorded or reserved being checked before another action.

**Takeaway:** “I can use people and automation without giving either a blank cheque.” This chapter must show enforcement at the action boundary, not merely an AI speech bubble, confidence score or an extra approval screen. Connecting an account does not enable autonomy. Human-approved financial actions are the MVP default; bounded automatic execution is a later, separately enabled mode.

### 3 — Connect control to good customer decisions

**Heading:**

> Put the customer right. With the full picture.

**Supporting copy:**

> Compare the evidence, earlier refunds and permitted remedies. Give your team a clear next step, with the reason behind it.

**Artefact:** use the existing three canonical cases as concise proof of distinct decisions. Damage: £96 refund or £33 known replacement cost (£28 goods + £5 shipping). Prior refund: £128 already refunded for the same item before another request is approved. Wrong item: £248 customer remedy and a separate potential warehouse recovery under its terms. Reveal which fact changes the route, then show the permitted action/result stage.

**Takeaway:** “It helps us make an appropriate decision and complete it.” Do not imply the cheapest option is always right, an earlier refund proves abuse, or a recommendation already executed. This scene is the gate applied to customer work; it should not repeat the whole gate diagram.

### 4 — Give recovery equal commercial weight

**Heading:**

> Help the customer. Pursue the money back.

**Supporting copy:**

> Reuse the investigation to build the right claim, submit through supported routes and follow it through—without making the customer wait for reimbursement.

**Artefact:** two independent tracks from the same evidence. The customer track ends in its actual resolution stage. The recovery track moves through applicable terms/claimant, evidence pack, supported submission, response/follow-up and collection check. Emphasise the next useful action at each stage. An early missing-photo request demonstrates why recovery starts during support, rather than after the evidence is gone.

**Takeaway:** “Sorting out the customer does not have to end our effort to recover the eligible cost.” Give the submission and follow-up work equal clarity to the pack. A polished folder alone is not the full benefit. Use a clearly labelled designed workflow or canonical illustrative sequence; never turn the pending SAMPLE-248 case into completed history.

### 5 — Show why the team uses it every day

**Heading:**

> Know what needs doing. And who's doing it.

**Supporting copy:**

> See the evidence to collect, claims to move forward and payments to check. Every open recovery keeps an owner and a next step.

**Artefact:** a focused Work view, using validated fixture rows or plainly labelled conceptual tasks: evidence required, ready for submission, response to review, payment to verify. Prioritisation must be visible through an actual supported deadline or a clearly named internal review date. Each row links to the same case and supporting records. Show a handoff retaining context, rather than three team panels reciting a pending state.

**Secondary beat in this chapter:**

> Already refunded? There may still be work worth doing.

> Review older imported cases for valid recovery routes, with the original terms and any existing claim in view.

The smaller history scene shows candidates and their evidence gaps, not a speculative “money found” total. Historical review and daily Work have different purposes, but both explain why Unauth matters beyond one newly opened ticket.

### 6 — Make the financial finish tangible

**Heading:**

> See what actually came back.

**Supporting copy:**

> Follow the claim through to payment or applied credit. Match it to the case and see what remains unrecovered.

**Artefact:** payment evidence visibly matched to its claim, then the resulting balance. Keep “Approved isn't paid” as the concise explanatory line beside the approval/receipt distinction. Use the canonical conditional £248 refund − £150 reconciled recovery = £98 unrecovered refund balance, with its exact label. Separate pending, received and reconciled states; no big ambiguous £150 at three different stages.

**Takeaway:** “Finance can trace the outcome, not just read that a claim was approved.” A credit issued is not yet an applied benefit. The simple landing can illustrate cash while naming applied credits in the copy; deeper allocation detail belongs in the product demonstration.

### 7 — Give the evidence a useful second life

**Heading:**

> Use what happened to improve what happens next.

**Supporting copy:**

> Trace recurring issues to the cases behind them. Preview a rule change before your team or connected AI starts using it.

**Artefact:** supported issue grouping → underlying cases → proposed rule → preview → authorised publication. Show an actual change in how a known case would be treated, with uncertain cases kept separate. Avoid fabricated trend charts or savings counters. A concentration suggests an investigation; it does not establish fault or root cause.

**Takeaway:** “We can learn deliberately from this work, instead of repeating the same investigation.” This is a compact substantive chapter, not a second analytics product or a promise of self-improving policy.

### 8 — Make adoption feel concrete

**Heading:**

> Keep the tools. Connect the work between them.

**Supporting copy:**

> Connect the records your setup can provide, agree your rules and owners, and choose which supported actions Unauth may carry out.

**Artefact:** a restrained source/action coverage view: store, helpdesk, **3PL warehouse**, **carrier delivery**, supplied agreements/documents and financial evidence. Distinguish records read from actions enabled. Show the actual integration/permission state; avoid an undifferentiated wall of logos. Financial evidence can be imported or reviewed manually—do not invent a bank/ERP connection.

Keep four concise FAQ topics: fit with current tools, human/AI control and current availability, evidence/eligibility for money back, and getting started. Preserve the existing pricing route and canonical commercial terms. Do not reintroduce removed repetitive fee claims.

### 9 — Close on the complete job

> From customer request to money accounted for.

Primary action remains **See it in action** with accurate fictional-desktop-walkthrough context; secondary action remains pricing. The walkthrough must eventually demonstrate the promises used to sell it. Until execution and AI are part of that demonstration, its current supervised scope must be clear.

## 6. Keep 3PL and carrier evidence distinct throughout

| Question | 3PL / warehouse | Carrier / delivery |
|---|---|---|
| What might this record establish? | What was picked, packed or handed over, where evidenced | Transit events, delivery status and available delivery proof |
| What would the merchant recognise? | Item/SKU, quantity, warehouse event, available packing image or acknowledgement | Tracking reference, event timeline, delivery scan, available photo/signature |
| What is the recovery question? | Does the applicable warehouse agreement support this claim, and through which route? | Does the shipment/account/service agreement support a claim, and who can submit? |
| What must not be implied? | Every 3PL supplies photos, weight, or acknowledged fault | A delivery scan alone proves the customer received the goods or establishes liability |

These are record families, not a universal division of liability. A 3PL or intermediary may be the correct route for a carrier claim. The handoff event joins the investigations without making them interchangeable.

The canonical wrong-item case can prove the warehouse story. Its missing carrier record must remain missing. **Do not make absence of carrier data the only depiction of carrier value.** In the platform chapter, show a labelled conceptual carrier timeline and the question it answers. For a later concrete carrier case, create a reviewed fixture with shipment events, uncertainty, applicable country/service/account terms, eligible claimant, required evidence, deadline basis and outcome state. Until then, show no invented scan, carrier fault, claim entitlement or amount. Keep the three existing case examples; a lost-parcel example is a candidate to validate, not an automatic fourth tab.

## 7. How this stays concise and visually substantial

The distinction is **few words per idea, enough ideas to explain the product**. Stop using whole-page word count as the primary design objective. Neither the old 1,000-word cap nor the later 650–800 target justifies dropping a buying reason. Measure reading burden as well as word count.

- Use one H1 and a small number of strong H2 chapters; “more heroes” means more meaningful visual chapters, not repeated opening screens and competing CTAs.
- Usually give a chapter one short headline, one 20–35-word explanation and one dominant artefact. This is a working discipline, not a reason to omit meaning or qualifications.
- Start with a wide before/after composition, then vary scale: shared gate, customer decision, two-track recovery, daily queue, financial finish and compact learning sequence. Avoid nine identical split panels or nested card grids.
- Keep the source objects and case identity visually consistent as they reappear, while changing the question each chapter answers. Do not repeat the same policy and £150 ceiling in every section.
- Let short status/role labels establish state; place one fictional or designed-workflow label per coherent illustration. Important availability cannot live only in a closed FAQ.
- On mobile, put headline and one sentence directly above the artefact. The two-track recovery must remain visibly independent; do not accidentally stack it into a dependency chain.
- An optional before/after control or brief transition can demonstrate the change, but both states and all main benefits must remain intelligible without animation, hover or tab exploration. Do not pin scrolling merely to create drama.
- Preserve the genuine hero, Unauth type/palette and existing public geometry as the visual baseline. This brief specifies storytelling roles; a future composition pass must verify them against the supplied Ramp references.

The proposed headline/subhead sequence is short enough to skim. Labels and product evidence then provide depth. If it is too long in render, remove a repeated representation before removing a distinct merchant benefit.

## 10. What must be true before calling the story successful

Review the proposal for these observable outcomes before a new implementation:

1. A headline-only skim communicates joined-up work, human/AI control, customer help, eligible recovery, daily ownership, verified money and learning.
2. A merchant recognises an actual repeated task that goes away or becomes easier. “It looks clean” is insufficient.
3. Each chapter visibly answers **what goes in, what Unauth does, what changes for me** without requiring a paragraph to decode the artefact.
4. A reader can distinguish a warehouse packing issue from a delivery issue, and understand why either may need different evidence and routes.
5. The gate shows both human discretion and a constrained AI action path; it never suggests every external tool is intercepted.
6. Customer resolution visibly continues independently of recovery. A claim pack is not the end of the recovery story.
7. The daily queue and historical review show recurring operational usefulness without inventing guaranteed opportunities.
8. A reader can distinguish eligible amount, approval and actual collection, and knows what the remaining balance means.
9. The product remains recognisably broader than refund review while staying within the committed spec.
10. The current offer and intended capability are understandable on the page itself.

For the next merchant review, ask readers to explain the changed work using their own process, identify what Unauth would add to their current tools, name what their team/AI controls, and identify what the financial scene proves. Ask them to rank the three cases and describe any recurring case missing from them. Keep the earlier minimum-9 acceptance ambition, but do not award it from a document, word count or our own confidence. The cold-read and real-process evidence is still required.

**Research conclusion:** bring back breadth through distinct merchant benefits and demonstrable connections. Keep individual explanations short. The story should make the whole product feel necessary before asking the visitor to inspect a single claim.
